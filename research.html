Where Chatham's real archives live

This site is a starting point. Every documented house chain, family file and local history monograph across Chatham Borough and Chatham Township rests on the work of our joint library and two volunteer historical societies.

📚

Library of the Chathams & The Fishawack Papers

Located at 214 Main Street (opened 1924; joint municipal library since 1974), the Library's New Jersey Room holds print Chatham city directories, microfilm of The Chatham Press (1897+), historic maps, and all 14 bound volumes (~5,100 pages) of The Fishawack Papers—compiled by over 425 local contributors since 1972 and searchable free online alongside digitized issues of The Chatham Courier (1946–present).

Copyrighted newspaper scans and local histories are linked directly from this site rather than reproduced.

Chatham & Chatham Township Historical Societies

The Chatham Historical Society (founded 1924, headquartered at the Library of the Chathams) and the Chatham Borough Historic Preservation Commission maintain house files, walking-tour histories and landmark surveys for the Borough. In the Township, the Chatham Township Historical Society operates the 1860 Red Brick Schoolhouse Museum at 24 Southern Boulevard, preserving farm, school, Madame Bey, Green Village and Great Swamp archives.

Both societies welcome researchers, memberships and historic photographs of Chatham homes.

Step by step

How to research your Chatham house

Whether your home sits on an 18th-century East Main Street lot, a 1920s commuter street or a former Township nursery tract:

  1. Check municipality, block & lot

    Confirm whether your property lies in Chatham Borough (incorporated 1897) or Chatham Township (incorporated 1806), and note your Morris County tax Block and Lot. Tax-assessor "year built" dates are helpful clues for post-1920 houses, though often rounded for 19th-century homes.

  2. Search The Fishawack Papers

    Use the Library of the Chathams' A–Z surname and subject indexes to The Fishawack Papers (14 volumes, ~5,100 pages). Hundreds of Chatham houses, farms, greenhouses, schools and founding families have dedicated essays inside.

  3. Compare the 1901, 1909 & 1921 Sanborn maps

    For Chatham Borough addresses, our House Story lookup automatically pulls matching plates from the public-domain 1901, August 1909 and 1921 Sanborn Fire Insurance surveys. Watch for early street renumbering—the 1901 and 1909 surveys note "arbitrary numbers used"—and trace changes in porches, carriage barns and glass greenhouses.

  4. Layer the 19th-century Morris County atlases

    Lightfoot & Geil's 1853 wall map, F. W. Beers's 1868 Atlas of Morris County, Robinson's 1887 Atlas and A. H. Mueller's 1910 Atlas print actual landowner names directly beside Main Street houses and rural Chatham Township farmsteads.

  5. Walk through directories & local newspapers

    Search The Chatham Courier (1946–present) online via the Library's Digifind-IT portal for real-estate transfers, building permits and neighborhood news, then consult print Chatham city directories and The Chatham Press (founded 1897) microfilm in the New Jersey Room at 214 Main Street.

  6. Check HABS & Register nominations

    During 1936–1938 federal Historic American Buildings Survey architects measured and photographed colonial Day family houses on Main Street and Elmwood Avenue. Properties in the Chatham Historic District (NJRHP 1973) or on the National Register have detailed architectural statements of significance.

  7. Trace the deed chain in Morristown

    With your Block, Lot and mid-20th-century owner's name, search Grantor/Grantee books at the Morris County Clerk's Office in Morristown (deeds recorded from 1785 onward; remember that before 1806 Chatham deeds were recorded under Morris or Hanover Township).

  8. Match the people in U.S. Census schedules

    Federal census sheets (1830–1950) and New Jersey state censuses (1855–1915) list every household member's occupation—from Passaic miller or rose grower to Lackawanna engineer. Note that 1810–1880 Chatham Township schedules also include Madison and Florham Park.

How this site works

Facts, stories, privacy & licensing

Facts. Every dated place, census figure and historical event comes from one of the sources listed below, with direct source links on every card.

House records & privacy. House pages combine four open sources when available: matching sheets of the public-domain 1901, 1909 and 1921 Sanborn maps, Library of Congress HABS & Gottscho-Schleisner photographs, curated historical notes, and brief text excerpts from pre-1951 issues of The Chatham Courier (1946–1950) linking straight to the Library of the Chathams viewer. To respect personal privacy, we never index post-1950 residents or attach street addresses to living people.

Images. Every image stored in this repository is verified public domain (USGS, Library of Congress HABS/Gottscho, U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, Sanborn pre-1929, CC0) or Creative Commons licensed with full creator and license attribution. Copyrighted collections are linked at their home archives.

Stories & illustrations. Narrative era overviews and the SVG Time Machine panorama are artistic syntheses designed to bring Chatham's history to life. Pre-1899 municipal boundaries on Explore the Map are reconstructed from Census TIGER polygons following John P. Snyder's boundary history.

Unofficial. This is an independent, non-commercial fan project with no official affiliation to Chatham Borough, Chatham Township, the Library of the Chathams or the local historical societies.
