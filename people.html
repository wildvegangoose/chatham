Notable people

Printers, illustrators, champions & conservationists

People connected to Chatham Borough and Chatham Township—from Revolutionary printer Shepard Kollock and illustrator A. B. Frost to Madame Hranoush Bey, the world boxing champions who trained on River Road, and modern writers, musicians, scientists and Olympians. Where a published historic record gives a pre-1951 address for someone no longer living, you can jump straight to that spot's story. Living people and post-1950 residents are listed without street addresses.

Born before 1850

JD

John Day

1675–1752Business & Building

Early colonial settler from Newark who acquired land along the Passaic River crossing around 1710–1720 and built the span that gave the village its colonial name, John Day's Bridge.

Settled at the Passaic crossing c. 1710–1720

JM

Jacob Morrell

1728–1814Business & Building

Pre-Revolutionary Main Street merchant and landowner whose c. 1740 homestead hosted General George Washington in late August 1781 on the eve of the Yorktown march.

Lived and ran a general store in Chatham

⌂ Lived at 63 Main Street (from c. 1740s; Chatham Historical Society)
NB

Nathaniel Bonnel

1731–1809Business & Building

Colonial miller, farmer and Revolutionary patriot descended from the Elizabethtown associates; built the c. 1750 plantation homestead in the Stanley section.

Settled on Watchung Avenue c. 1750

⌂ Lived at 32 Watchung Avenue (from c. 1750; Explore Chatham)
EB

Elias Boudinot

1740–1821Public Life & Law

President of the Continental Congress (1782–1783), Commissary General of Prisoners under Washington, U.S. Congressman and Director of the U.S. Mint; moved his family to Chatham during the Revolutionary War behind the Watchung line.

Wartime resident of Chatham

SK

Shepard Kollock

1750–1839Writing & Journalism

Continental Army artillery lieutenant and 'rebel printer' who established The New-Jersey Journal at Day's Tavern in Chatham on February 16, 1779, printing patriot news, almanacs and books throughout the war.

Published The New-Jersey Journal in Chatham, 1779–1783

VB

Vincent Boisaubin

1755–1834Public Life & Law

Guadeloupe-born French nobleman and officer of King Louis XVI's Garde du Corps who fled the French Revolution, befriended Lafayette and built Greek Revival Boisaubin Manor ('Beau Plain') in Chatham Township.

Lived in Chatham Township until his death in 1834

⌂ Lived at 95 Treadwell Avenue (c. 1822–1834; National Register nomination)
CW

Capt. William Day

1756–1825Public Life & Law

Morris County militia officer who fought at the Battle of Springfield (June 1780) and farmed a 100-acre Main Street homestead with his wife Nancy Bonnel.

Lifelong Chatham patriot and farmer

⌂ Lived at 70 Main Street (1780–1825; Chatham Historical Society)
DJ

Dr. John C. Budd

1762–1845Science & Engineering

Prominent post-Revolutionary Chatham physician and civic figure whose Main Street lane—Budd Lane—later became North Passaic Avenue.

Practiced medicine in Chatham

RJ

Rev. Joseph Meeker Ogden

1804–1884Public Life & Law

Princeton Theological Seminary graduate who served forty-five years (1828–1873) as pastor of the Village Church of Chatham, later renamed Ogden Memorial Presbyterian Church in his honor.

Pastor in Chatham, 1828–1873; died in 1884

⌂ Lived at 186 Main Street (1848–1873; National Register nomination)
CM

Charles M. Lum

1830–1909Business & Building

19th-century drover and landowner who brought cattle on foot from Sussex County to Chatham; his West Main Street tract later held some of the Borough's largest rose greenhouses.

Lived on West Main Street

⌂ Lived at 492 Main Street (from c. 1860; Explore Chatham)
GS

George Shepard Page

1838–1892Business & Building

Industrialist, coal-tar manufacturer and angler who developed the paper and felt mills along the Passaic River and named the Stanley section of Chatham for his mother's family.

Developed Chatham's Stanley mill village

AM

Aaron Montgomery Ward

1844–1913Business & Building

Founder of Montgomery Ward & Co. (1872), inventor of the general mail-order catalog for rural America and tireless legal defender of Chicago's Grant Park as 'forever open, clear and free.'

Born in Chatham on February 17, 1844

FH

Frederick Harvey Lum

1848–1905Public Life & Law

Civic leader who served as Village President of Chatham Village and became the first Mayor of Chatham Borough upon its incorporation on March 1, 1897.

First Mayor of Chatham Borough (1897)

RS

Raymond St. James Perrin

1849–1915Writing & Journalism

Philosophical author (The Religion of Philosophy) and Bridge Street varnish manufacturer who laid out Perrin, Bonnel and St. James streets in the Stanley section.

Lived in the Stanley section until his death in 1915

⌂ Lived at 70 River Road (late 1800s–1915; Explore Chatham)

Born 1850–1899

AB

A. B. Frost

1851–1928Film, TV & Art

Celebrated Golden Age American illustrator, comics pioneer and sporting painter (famous for over 100 illustrations of Br'er Rabbit and Uncle Remus), who bought Boisaubin Manor in 1890 and named his country estate 'Moneysunk.'

Lived at 'Moneysunk' in Chatham Township, 1890–1906

⌂ Lived at 95 Treadwell Avenue (1890–1906; National Register nomination)
JW

Joseph Wallace Ogden

1853–1919Business & Building

Investment banker and founder of the New York municipal-bond house J. W. Ogden & Company, born in the Main Street Presbyterian parsonage.

Born in Chatham, 1853

⌂ Lived at 186 Main Street (born here, 1853; National Register nomination)
LB

Lyman B. Coddington

1858–1946Business & Building

Commercial rose grower and hybridizer whose greenhouses shipped prize cut roses across the East and introduced famous patented varieties including 'President Herbert Hoover.'

Pioneered Chatham-area commercial rose growing

EE

Edward Everett Bruen

1859–1938Public Life & Law

Descendant of Chatham's colonial Bruen family, real-estate developer and first Mayor of East Orange (1899–1905).

Born in Chatham, 1859

FL

Frank L. Kelley

1864–1936Public Life & Law

Downtown Main Street merchant and Mayor of Chatham Borough from 1903 to 1915, presiding over the 1906 raised-crosswalk speed bumps and the 1914 Lackawanna Railroad elevation.

Mayor of Chatham Borough, 1903–1915

AR

Anthony Ruzicka

1868–1949Business & Building

Horticulturist and wholesale rose grower who modernized Samuel Lum's West Main Street greenhouses and operated major glasshouse ranges during Chatham's Rose Capital heyday.

Chatham rose grower

AE

Ambrose Ely Vanderpoel

1875–1940Writing & Journalism

Attorney, genealogist and historian whose exhaustive 1921 volume History of Chatham, New Jersey preserved primary Revolutionary War dispatches, maps and early family histories.

Chatham's foundational historian

LP

Lynda Phillips Lum

1878–1963Public Life & Law

One of early Chatham's first college-educated women and founding librarian when the brick Library of the Chathams opened in Memorial Park in 1924.

First librarian of the 1924 Library of the Chathams

WS

Walter Scott Story

1879–1955Writing & Journalism

Prolific magazine writer and author of historical adventure books for young readers including Skinny Harrison, Adventurer and Boy Heroes of the Sea.

Lived in Chatham Borough

HB

Hranoush "Madame Bey" Sidky Bey

1881–1942Sports

Constantinople-educated mezzo-soprano, polyglot and former Ottoman diplomat's wife who ran world boxing's legendary training camp on River Road from 1923 to 1942, training Gene Tunney, Max Schmeling, Primo Carnera, James J. Braddock and Freddie Steele.

Founded Madame Bey's Boxing Camp in Chatham Township (1923–1942)

⌂ Lived at 516 River Road (1923–1942; Summit & Chatham Twp. Historical Societies)
